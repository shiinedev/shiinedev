Most design systems do not fail because of bad component APIs. They fail because nobody owns them after launch week.

## Three things that keep a system alive

- A token layer thin enough that people actually use it.
- A single obvious place to request changes.
- A recurring ritual where drift gets reviewed and folded back in.

## Start smaller than feels responsible

Colour, type, spacing, and four or five components cover most of a product surface. Everything else can wait until a second real use case appears.

Treat the system as a product with users. If designers and engineers route around it, that is feedback about the system, not about them.
